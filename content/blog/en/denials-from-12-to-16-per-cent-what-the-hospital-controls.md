Between 2023 and 2024, initial claim denials at hospitals affiliated to Anahp rose from 11.89% to 15.89% of billing. In the same period, payer delinquency went from 49.96% to 61.53% of outstanding amounts. For a mid-sized hospital, those are two leaks that add up.

## What sits in the process

Incomplete coding, expired authorisations, mismatch between records and the bill, materials without traceability. All of that is avoidable and responds to routine: checking before submission, an indicator per payer and feedback to the clinical team.

## What sits in the contract

Payment terms without penalties, no rule for appealing denials, price lists without adjustment and bundles with open scope. Here process does not solve it; renegotiation does. And it starts with knowing how much each payer represents in revenue and how late it pays.

## The reimbursement model has changed

Almost two thirds of hospitals already operate outside pure fee-for-service, with global daily rates and bundles. Without a known cost per procedure, a bundle is a bet.

## Three numbers for this week

Denials per payer, average collection period per payer and share of revenue concentrated in the three largest. With them, the conversation with the payer moves from complaint to negotiation.

*Sources: Observatório Anahp 2025 and 2026; Futuro da Saúde (Apr 2025).*
