International groups entering Brazil's healthcare market tend to underestimate the same set of things: the licence is municipal, the professional council has more power than it seems, revenue flows through payers that pay late, and health data is sensitive by law. Five questions avoid most surprises.

## 1. Who can own, and who must sign

Ownership of clinics by non-professionals is possible, but technical responsibility belongs to a professional registered with the council. Without it there is no licence. The corporate structure must reflect that from the start.

## 2. Which licence the real activity requires

The sanitary licence is issued by the municipality for the declared activity. Surgery, hospitalisation and injectables raise the level of requirements. In São Paulo, the municipal licence is a prerequisite for operating and renovations require an approved plan before the works.

## 3. Where the revenue comes from

In hospitals and laboratories, 60% to 90% of revenue comes from payers, with average denials above 15% and rising delinquency. The payer contract is the most important asset and the worst read.

## 4. What the council allows you to advertise

Medical, dental and veterinary advertising each have their own rules. Marketing that works in the home country can be an ethics violation here.

## 5. Where the data lives and who is accountable

The data authority gained shutdown powers in 2026 and prioritised health data. Records, reports and images require a data-protection programme before the first patient.

The investor readiness answers all five in four to eight weeks, with a risk register and a closing checklist.
