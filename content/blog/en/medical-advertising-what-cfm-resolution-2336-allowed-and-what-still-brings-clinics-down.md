Since March 2024, CFM Resolution 2,336/2023 allows physicians in Brazil to advertise prices, show equipment and publish before-and-after images for educational purposes. For clinics it is a real marketing opening. It is also the reason behind many recent ethics complaints.

## What remains forbidden

Promising or guaranteeing results, sensationalism, advertising a specialty without the RQE registration, comparison with colleagues and using a patient's image without specific consent. And one point that goes unnoticed: the physician is liable for third-party content they share.

## In veterinary medicine, new rules since October 2025

CFMV Resolution 1,649/2025 follows the same logic: it bans prices for procedures that require assessment, forbids guaranteed results and treats reposts as the professional's own content. Veterinary clinics' social media needs an audit, not intuition.

## Positioning without exposure

A clinic's brand is not built with promotions. It is built with a differential stated in one sentence, a routine of Google reviews, content that teaches instead of promising and a website that schedules without friction. When those exist, advertising a price becomes a detail.

## Quick check

Open the clinic's last twenty posts and mark those that promise results, cite a specialty without RQE or show a patient without a consent form. The number tells the size of the job.

*References: CFM Res. 2,336/2023; CFMV Res. 1,649/2025. Confirm the rules are in force before deciding.*
