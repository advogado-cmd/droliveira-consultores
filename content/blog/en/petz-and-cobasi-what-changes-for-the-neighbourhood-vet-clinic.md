Brazil's antitrust authority approved the merger between Petz and Cobasi in December 2025, with the divestment of 26 stores in São Paulo. The resulting group bills around R$ 7 billion, runs 515 stores and concentrates 112 veterinary clinics and 15 hospitals. For the neighbourhood clinic, the question is not whether the chain will arrive. It is what to do when it does.

## Where the chain wins

Prices of food and medicines, extended hours, national marketing and capital for equipment. Competing on those four points is losing slowly.

## Where the independent clinic wins

The relationship with the owner, continuity of the same veterinarian, speed of decision and local reputation built over years. Those assets only count when they are visible: a Google profile with recent reviews, an owner base activated by reminders, simple scheduling and a service the chain does not offer (a specialty, hospitalisation, exotic animals).

## The detail that decides

Purchase multiples for mid-sized clinics fell from 7 to 8 times EBITDA to 4 to 6 times. Buyers pay for demonstrable management: monthly indicators, electronic records, registration in order and service invoices issued. A clinic that cannot prove what it bills is worth less, whether to sell or to resist.

## First step

Measure three numbers: share of owners active in the last twelve months, average ticket per visit and number of Google reviews against the nearest competitor. The plan starts where the number is worst.

*Sources: Agência Brasil (Dec 2025); EloVetNet, M&A review for the first quarter of 2026.*
