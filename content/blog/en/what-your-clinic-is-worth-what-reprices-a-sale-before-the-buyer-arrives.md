Brazil's healthcare sector is consolidating quickly: hospitals and laboratories closed 80% more deals in the first quarter of 2025 than the year before, and the veterinary market saw its two largest chains merge. On the selling side, price depends less and less on reputation and more and more on demonstrable management.

## What the buyer looks at first

Registration and licences: activity code, corporate purpose, permits and technical officer. Contracts with payers and suppliers: term, adjustment, change of control. Trademark: registered or not. People: dependence on the founding partner and labour liabilities. Data: electronic records and a data-protection programme. None of that appears on the balance sheet, and all of it reprices.

## The seller's due diligence

Running your own diligence before the buyer's changes the negotiation: what they will find, you find first, fix what can be fixed and present the rest with a plan and a deadline. An organised data room and numbered pending items save months.

## Indicative valuation

EBITDA multiples vary by segment, size and quality of management. A mid-sized veterinary clinic today trades between 4 and 6 times; two years ago, between 7 and 8. The difference between the top and the bottom of the range is, largely, what a prior diligence fixes.

## On the buying side

A risk register across the fourteen fronts, change-of-control clauses, non-transferable licences and a first-hundred-days plan. What blocks or reprices the deal must surface before signing, not after.

*Sources: KPMG/InfoMoney (Jun 2025); EloVetNet (2026); Agência Brasil (Dec 2025). Figures are market references, not a case valuation.*
